export const rollDeferGap = function(e, t = Math.random) {
    const n = Math.min(2, e);
    const o = Math.max(n, e);
    return n + Math.floor(t() * (o - n + 1));

* insights — mine, unruled; the quest's =* the steal= is the short form

1. One beat, two callers. A pure step with =now= and =random= in =ctx= runs the same on the screen or the daemon, and replays from a seed. Q14 then decides only who calls it.
2. Flags are the spine. A run persists a grow-only set, counters and a pose; quest progress, inventory, dialog choice and who stands where are derived. =data= stays small, and law 5 holds without a blob.
3. Effects and events are separate outputs. Effects go to the screen; events (new flags) go to the quests. One reducer, two consumers, and no screen code in the rules.
4. Their battle never writes memory and nothing is spaced. Ruling 3 makes every graded answer a =literal.review=, so every fight is practice — a gain over theirs, not a copy.
5. Their memory model is a three-state ladder of counters. Here it is a reading of =literal.assess()= (law 3); their gates by count become gates by status over a symbol set.
6. Their terms bind to content three ways with no shared id. Here one binding is enough: a literal slug or a symbol set on every line, item and encounter.
7. Inventory is a difference of counters, so there is no inventory store to keep in sync.
8. A quest is data: activation over flags, steps triggered by flag prefixes, rewards idempotent by key. Nothing about a quest is ever stored.
9. The world is rebuilt on map entry from content + flags, so a buffer never holds NPC or monster instances.
10. Their dialogue is pre-rendered (2.2 MB of captions); one guardian riddle is graded by an LLM. Here the cortex speaks (ruling 4), and the latency a beat tolerates is still owed (work 3).
11. Their two question loops share no code: a clocked one with no memory, and an unclocked one with memory. One loop that is clocked AND reviews is the obvious steal, and Q10 rules the clock.
