** the address law — two tiers, no chain (beef, 2026-09-19: /"serve remote origin feels like one too much?! how can serve === remote??!"/ · /"no copy. confusing af"/)
Before: ORIGIN → SERVE = =${ORIGIN}/path= → REMOTE = =${SERVE}=. Three keys per addressable thing, two of them copies of the first. The chain only held on localhost; the moment a proxy sat in front, REMOTE stopped being a copy and the default lied.

After — every addressable thing has at most TWO keys, and they answer two different questions:
- =VIVA_<PROCESS>_ORIGIN= — where the process BINDS. scheme + host + port. The runtime is the only process that binds in an instance; a service attaches INSIDE the runtime's tree, so it has no ORIGIN of its own.
- =PUBLIC_VIVA_<THING>_REMOTE= — where a consumer REACHES it. Full URL, path included. A LITERAL default (=http://localhost:2502/attached/…=), never =${…}= of another key. Equal to the bind address on a laptop; different behind compose networks, tunnels, proxies. Two truths, two keys, no default pretending they are one.
- =*_SERVE= keys DIE. The path a service attaches at (=/attached/process/lighthouse/<slug>=) is structural — the attach slot — not configuration. The runtime derives a service's serve URL as =ORIGIN + slot=; nobody types it into an env. =integrate.js:28,61= keeps reading =runtime.statics.serve= (a thunk over ORIGIN now), the service's =statics.serve= thunk is gone.
