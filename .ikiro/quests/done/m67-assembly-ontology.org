* rulings

1. *kind is the ontology key.* USD's model hierarchy minus =group=, plus =step= (ours; USD has no noun for it). =subcomponent= dropped: zero uses across Kitchen_set · OpenChessSet · hardware. A literal's ONE TOPOGRAPHICAL symbol IS its kind; the runtime stamps it as =ontology=.
2. *format.* glTF 2.0 is the file and the wire. USD's composition model — reference · over · class · instanceable — is the ROW model, never written as .usda. Placement is NOT a literal: it is the xformOp on the reference.
3. *three as engine, threlte as binding.* MEASURED: =bundle.svelte= as-is FAILED on threlte (47× /"=$bindable()= can only be used inside a =$props()= declaration"/, first at =@threlte/core/dist/components/T/T.svelte:25=). Cause: =svelte-preprocess@6.0.3='s TS pass ran at its default old =target= and downlevelled threlte's rest-destructure into a =__rest= helper, so =$bindable()= landed outside =$props()=. LANDED, both lines of =subsystems/typology/gestalten/bundle/svelte.js=:

Everything shipped so far is RAW three, not threlte. beef's 2024 threlte + rapier prototype: =quests/roadmap/threlte-rapier-drapes.org=.
4. *tool-first, at the mode.* A mode's capability is a =tools= verb; the aperture WRAPS it when the App needs a door. Blender headless is the mode's (=blend.js= + =harvest.py=, media's =ffmpeg.js= precedent); the browser never writes. Promote to the domain when a second mode wants it. Governs a MODE only — see 14.
5. *Two packages.* =@assembly= is the base (domain + topologies + the importer); =@droneaid= keeps the product (its topography, its guide, its instance).
6. *A step is a layer; an assembly is a layer stack.* USD's LayerStack: the stage at step N is sublayers 0…N composed, later stronger. =COMPOSED{nodes}= + =JOINED{joints}= are the traits of a LAYER — an assembly and a step both carry them. An assembly adds =SEQUENCED{steps}=; =resolve(slug, {upto: k})= folds its own layer then steps 0…k. No intermediate product is ever a row: "the frame after step 5" is derived. =TRANSITIONED= is dead — order is the sequence.
7. *Operations are entity and repository methods; tools use them.* beef: /"some of these arent tools. they are entity operations! they are methods on entity or repository."/ The entity READS (=row.nodes · joints · steps · layer=), the repository is the ONLY WRITER (=component · assembly · step · place · join · sequence=, maintains =uses=) and QUERIES (=closure=) and FOLDS (=resolve · bom=). The folds are pure functions in =resolve.js= over plain rows — no em, no flush — and the App imports the same file to fold the dataspace's rows in the browser. Measured: =v= has NO =tuple=, NO =lazy= (beef: /"not sure v.lazy array is real"/ — it is not), so nodes are FLAT, glTF's own shape: one array, hierarchy by =parent= path. TRS is glTF's node TRS verbatim.
8. *Three topologies, one per kind.* Ruling 6 puts the seam between the two layers (assembly · step) and the one with geometry (component). Each topology mints its own families.
9. *The topography is =@droneaid/topography/model=, version =0.0.0=* — beef: /"droneaid model.0000"/. The model IS the topography; =pt10= is the frame's name, not the product's.
10. *Rows are metres, Y-up — glTF's own law.* Converted at harvest; =SOURCED.scale= remembers Blender's factor for the round trip. Embed bakes SCALE only, never rotation: one =arm= component, four nodes with rotation.
11. *No OVERRIDDEN trait.* Later layer wins by path (LIVRPS, local opinion): a node re-declared with only =name= + what changes is an over; a joint re-declared by =fastener= with =torque: "tight"= is step 13. Removal is USD's word: =active: false=. =group= is a node with neither =component= nor =assembly= — never a row.
12. *=COUNTED{qty}= is the kit.* What the box ships. What the build uses is =bom()=, derived. The check is the two disagreeing.
13. *The domain declares what it hardcodes; the owner is the caller's.* beef: /"whatever the assembly domain hardcodes/requires in terms of traits/entities/ontologies/topologies must be part of the domain declaration itself."/ Education is blind to its topologies because it never mints a literal; assembly must, so it cannot be blind to its KINDS — =KINDS= is a private table in =Literal.ts= read by =mint= and nowhere else. Chess's =owner(kind)= lookup is GONE: every writer takes the owner from the caller (the topography being edited, or the mode that mints). The domain never looks a topology up.
14. *The domain follows education; ruling 4 governs a mode.* On a domain the doors ARE the verbs — one repository call each — and a tool rides its door through =daemon.call= carrying the caller; the App calls the same door over the wire. Two seats, not one rule. The barrel is =entities · schematics · aperture · tools · manifest= and nothing else (§the barrel).
15. *The bin IS the buffer.* A phase is derived off the bin — a loose object is intake, a member is interpret, annotated is annotate, minted is embed — never stored. =bin N= in the header IS the buffer index.
16. *The importer mints under itself; write publishes.* Rows in the daemon are the import mode's (a row's owner is a single mode); the topography's copy is what =/write= puts on disk and what a fresh daemon installs. Two owners, one slug — by design; =/literals= says whose. OPEN: beef's word after the first live write.
17. *No symbol is guessed.* =annotate= proposes slug · name · count; material and source are the hand's or the agent's, from the vocabulary the harness lists.
18. *Every mode has a ground, seated by paladin.* =resolve.ground= seats a kernel entry at =<daemon.mountpoint>/mode_<type>_<slug>= unless the entry names one; a declared-but-blank one stays a REQUIRED fault the doctor names. beef: /"the mountpoint should be on EVRERY mode by default as a function of paladin doing its thing."/ · /"paladin wires up the environment"/ · /"no overwrite needed. no custom variable."/ The fallthrough is now ledger → instance → mountpoint → daemon/service → mode, each overridable, an override re-rooting everything below. Record: =roadmap/mountpoint-fallthrough.org=.
19. *The daemon starts empty.* A topography ships its vocabulary and its pages; its rows are what the importer writes into =dataset/literals/=. Nothing authored by hand or by a script rides a dataset — and an upsert never deletes, so a shipped row is permanent. beef, on the 48 =harvest.js= rows the kernel installed: /"why is there data in at all?!??!!?"/ · /"makes no sense. shouldnt be"/.

20. *A placement is a literal.* beef: /"is the nodes maybe a literal? It sounds like that would make sense. Um, because both the the component or assembly, though they are specific things, and the placement of something is also a specific thing. So I don't see why that shouldn't be a dedicated literal. And group still, yeah, it doesn't make sense to me also."/ A placement's slug is =<layer>.<path>= (=/= → =.=), so the same layer, parent and name again IS the same row — an over by construction. =ref= is never absent: a placement that places nothing is unrepresentable, which is what killed =group=. Supersedes ruling 2's "placement is NOT a literal" and ruling 11's group clause.
21. *Part swallows component and assembly.* beef: /"let's call them assembly and placement. Or no, I like part and placement. Part. Placement and step where part and placement are both part of the assembly ontology and step will be its own thing later for the guide tutorial."/ The two differed only by which field was absent — a union by absence — and a bought sub-assembly (a motor you order AND whose interior you know) was inexpressible under either. =COMPOSED · NODE · JOINT · LAYER= gone; a layer's contents are the placements that name it; =JOINED= rides the fastener's placement. Rulings 6 and 8 read with =part= for =assembly=. Two ways to nest, never blurred: =parent= is structure inside ONE layer; =ref= naming a part with an interior grafts that composition under the placement's path.
22. *Every import is its own.* beef: /"I don't think the import should overwrite anything that we already have in the system. They should be standalone and for the rest... No, yeah, no, no existing parts. Each import is its own. And if we ever want this existing part substitution, then I think we can add that on later. Go."/ =settings.into= names the root part; a slug the mode already owns is suffixed. The top level is derived — a part nothing places is a root (beef: /"but now our commit misses the overarching top level container. what should that be?"/).
23. *Two manifests name a daemon's mounts.* The registry's =instances/<slug>/daemon.js= AND the installed =~/.viva/instances/<slug>/daemon.js=; a topology renamed in one and not the other 404s the boot — measured on beef's watcher: =[VIP] Module 404: {"owner":"@assembly","type":"topology","slug":"component"}=.
24. *A slug is unique. There is no owner.* beef: /"So absolutely slugs are unique. There's only one slug ever. And if there's a conflict in naming, then we need more annotation, more depth to our pieces. We might just number them, but the slugs are actually are truly unique. no separate owner, you clearly have not understood the way that vivalence's systems work. They might have one optionally in term, as a mode attached, but that is not something, there's no owner."/ Measured: the runtime's =Literal.mode= is optional (=entities/kernel/Literal.ts:73=, =mode?: Rel<ModeEntity>=) and its own upsert key is =unique(opt) { return { slug } }= (=:17=); the =(slug, mode)= db unique exists for one module kernelled twice (m53), a runtime concern. In this domain the slug IS the identity: every lookup is =findOne({ slug })=, =mint= refuses a slug anyone holds, =mode= is an annotation the minting mode may leave. The importer's =taken= set becomes every slug in the daemon, not the mode's. Supersedes ruling 13's "every writer takes the owner from the caller" and ruling 16's two-owners-one-slug; the aperture's =owner= input and the tools' no-owner pin (=tests/domain.test.js:30=) both go.

** the navigation standard — the implementer's call, beef to confirm

beef's design project ("Editor assembly recreation", =Assembly Editor.dc.html=) marks its own tree navigation a PLACEHOLDER and hands the standard to the implementer verbatim: /"NAVIGATION IS NOT SPECIFIED HERE — IMPLEMENTER'S CALL … beef found it counter-intuitive and it should NOT be copied as-is."/ It names six open questions: what a click means vs. what opens a subject · keyboard as the primary road · the expansion model · how a 36-placement fold stays legible · where multi-select lives · tree ↔ stage ↔ timeline reciprocity. The standard chosen, applied to all three panes:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
CURSOR and SELECTION are different things.
  cursor     one row, where the keyboard is. local to the view.
  selection  0..n slugs, on the buffer. what the inspector and the stage read.

A CLICK SELECTS. A selection NEVER changes the subject.
OPENING DESCENDS, and is a separate, explicit act — four ways in, one way out:
  Enter · o        open the cursor row          dbl-click a row     open it
  click its TAIL   ("6 placements" / "← arm")   dbl-click a mesh    open what it places
  a PLACEMENT opens WHAT IT PLACES (its ref) — so any layer of the hierarchy (an antenna, a
  camera, a motor bought assembled) becomes the subject on its own. A STEP opens its part
  folded upto itself. `open <slug>` on the line and the open tool land the same place.
  u · Backspace    up one. The header carries the trail as clickable crumbs.

j k ↑ ↓   walk the cursor          g G   first · last
l →       expand, else descend     h ←   collapse, else ascend
space x   toggle into selection    Esc   peel: dialog → notice → selection → up
/         the search               :     the line
[ ]       upto − +                 1-4   isolate ALL · PART · PLACEMENT · STEP

A selection arriving from OUTSIDE (a tool, a stage pick, a lane) auto-expands the tree to it
and moves the cursor there. Tree, stage and sequence read one cursor and one selection.
Legibility: the isolate filter, `upto`, and children that only walk when their branch is open.
#+END_EXAMPLE

Walked by beef 09-22 and corrected on the spot: /"i\'d want to be able to directly click into intermediate assemblies. like seeing everything in the antenna, camera or other placements. any layer of the hierarchy."/ Opening a placement had gone UP (to its layer) — it now goes DOWN (to its =ref=), the tail is a direct click in, the stage takes a double-click, a trail of crumbs and =u= / =Backspace= come back out, and =Esc='s last step is /up/ rather than a clear.
