* the law — so far

1. A subject runs and owns a directory. An entity that does not run is a file under =entities/=.
2. =<subject>/= holds =prototype.js= (the class) or =<Subject>.ts= (the mikro entity, or both when it is both) · =factory.js= (THE export: =(input, environment) → the live subject=, composing the lifecycle) · =lifecycle/{population,resolution,integration}.js= (phases; one phase = =lifecycle.js=, a file) · =traits/<NAME>.js= + =index.js= · =commands/= · =apertures/=.
3. Predicate directories hold what no subject owns: =entities/= · =prototypes/= · =gestalten/{belt,shard,tools}= · =schematics/=. =subsystems/typology= is predicative all the way.
4. Write at the owner; two owners → =gestalten/shard/=. A tool file is named by its object.
5. The typology export is predicative: a consumer asks for a KIND (=factories.mode= · =traits.mode.DATASET= · =entities.Mode=), never for a subject.
6. A lifecycle is exported for tests and never imported in use; use imports the factory.
7. A subject's trait vocabulary is declared once, in typology's schematic for that subject; a system's catalog implements a subset; a name with no schematic entry is not a trait.
8. The grammar is the lint, not the layout: every file has ONE role — subject · verb · adjective · object; the export is the predicates.
9. A factory is =(parentDie, …) → childDie=; a subject is made by its factory and by nothing else; the three deliverables land in order: the move, the factories, the vocabulary.
10. An entity stays an entity. A trait with a body on an entity lives at =entities/<entity>/<name>.js=; the beat that runs it belongs to the subject whose process it is. Subjecthood is per system: a terminal is an entity in the runtime and a subject in ghost.
