* the three rounds of one order

*Round one — the defaults that were not the problem.* I read /"remove all defaults"/ as a grep for =.default()= and swept: the schematics, the placeholders, the =pane= enum, the =~/Downloads= comment. All of it was worth cutting and none of it was what he saw. What he saw was thirty objects sitting in his bin on open.

*Round two — the mechanism.* Ruling 15 says the bin IS the buffer. A buffer row therefore *remembers*, and the importer's screen opens on the last thread. The thirty objects were the intake I had loaded during my own Chrome verification two folds earlier, written into beef's thread and handed back every time he opened it. Two buffer rows held one; I cleared both through the app's own =/clear=, then proved a fresh thread opens empty. Also landed: the buffer subscription is keyed on =buffer.id= and returns early without one — a filter with no id matches every buffer, and another buffer's bin would walk in as an import nobody asked for.

*Round three — the shape.* /"why is there shit about the mountpoint in the ui"/. The mountpoint is the daemon's business. The operator has a file. Everything the panel did — scan the mountpoint, list the blends, list prior intakes, name the intake directory — was the daemon's internals rendered as a form. =/upload= takes the bytes and gives back literals, and the four phase doors stay for the hand's corrections and the harness.

The three rounds are one failure with three chances to catch it: *I answered the sentence's nouns instead of its verb.* "Remove defaults" has a grep. "I want to import my own file" has a design.

* what the blank stage actually was

beef, in 113: /"the threejs rendering doesnt work … no parts are displaying. either from this fake unwanted import, nor from the existing data."/ Two halves, two causes, neither of them three.js:

- *The import half.* His buffer row read =pane: "data", selected: "FlightController00"= — his screenshot exactly. =populate()= returned early on =pane !== "import"= and nothing redrew on the way back. Reproduced by flipping my own theme to paper and looking at the same row. With the data tab deleted, the pane is gone and so is the branch.
- *The data half.* The 48 literals with no owner at all, whose GLBs had been moved to =bak/= when the instance was rebuilt. Nothing to render because the files were not there. The purge answered it.

The renderer *was* drawing the whole time — a wireframe drone at 0.6 scale, confirmed by zooming the screenshot rather than trusting the small view.

* the hot renderer

beef: /"renderer is going nuts"/ — Activity Monitor at 87.2 %, later measured at 570 %, two hours of CPU in fifty-five minutes of wall clock. Two causes, and only one was code:

1. The stage ran =requestAnimationFrame= unconditionally and rendered every frame whether or not anything had moved. Now =controls.update() || dirty= gates it: the camera's own damping keeps it alive while it moves, a scene change or resize marks it dirty, and an idle stage costs nothing.
2. The hot process was fifty-five minutes old — the tab *I* opened at 18:13 in the previous fold and never closed. It reconnected through three daemon restarts running a stale bundle. Every same-origin tab shares that renderer process, so my fresh tab froze with it and the live checks stalled twice before it died.

The second one has a rule and I had it: close the tabs you open. Written to memory as =feedback_close_verification_tabs= so the next hot renderer is suspected as a leftover tab before it is suspected as code.
