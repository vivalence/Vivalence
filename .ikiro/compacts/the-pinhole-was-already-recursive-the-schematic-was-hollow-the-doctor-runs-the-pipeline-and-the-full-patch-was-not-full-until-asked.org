* arc

** the pinhole was already the law; the schematic was the hole (rows 1–3)

beef's opening carried its own answer — /"unless we define every key to be a possible thunk. then we'd
have the paladin resolve this all at once recursively"/ — and the measurement said: that is what
=hydrate= does, and has done since the pinhole quest. So the ternary was never a thunk problem. It was
optionality with nowhere to live, because =v.primitives.instance= says =statics: v.record(v.string(),
v.unknown())= on every slot and an =unknown= cannot be filled, minted, or judged. The same hole explains
all three of his complaints at once: the =?:= (optionality hand-encoded per site, though the env schema
already says =.optional()= and its desc already says "no hallucinator"), =new Url(…)= (the type
hand-minted per site, though the codec sits unused in =signatures.js=), and =statics: {}= (defaults
hand-filled, though =Daemon.cast= already fills them — the recipes were carrying corpses). His
three-line restatement in row 3 is the design: /"solid schematics for what the config is expected to
be. we pinhole in paladin on in. and somewhere in the paladin where we know what each bit of config
should look like we validate/fill."/ The patch types what typology KNOWS (=serve= on
runtime/client/service, =remote= on the lighthouse) and leaves module-specific statics (nlp =remote=,
openrouter =models=) to a module-declared contract that does not exist yet — the trigger is the next
=new Url= in a =consume= block.

** the doctor decides where the throw lives (row 4)

The one fork the first patch carried was structural: the doctor mounts a bare recipe (blank =.env=) to
REPORT, so a =Url= codec that throws at mount would kill the doctor on a fresh instance. The fix I
proposed was two entry points — lenient =mount=, strict =settle= — and every caller would have to know
which it wanted. beef's question dissolved it: if the doctor runs the whole pipeline, misses must be
DATA (=instance.faults=), and the throw belongs only to whoever boots. That is the shape
=check.environment= already has (doctor reads rows; =run= and =init= read =check.wrong=), so slot
faults join a pattern instead of inventing one. Two measured facts then bent the implementation:
=Value.Decode= is Check-then-transform and refuses a value it already decoded, so a faulted slot stays
as hydrated and decode runs once; decode clones plain objects, so lighthouse inheritance moves AFTER
settle (=daemon.lighthouse ??= instance.lighthouse=) and the identity =lighthouse.test.js= pins
survives.

** dormancy, and why it stops at hallucinators (rows 1–2, 5)

The evidence that the ternary was a patch: =~/.viva/registry/education/instances/*/daemon.js= already
declares the flat array with three =.optional()= keys, and both SDKs construct on a =null= key, so
that recipe boots a cortex that advertises =has("speech")= with a dead faculty behind it. m48
decision 3 had accepted the ternary as "the documented way to cope"; m52 revokes it with one rule at
the pinhole — a hallucinator whose secret is blank is dormant, dropped from the roster, named on the
instance, still in the doctor's record. It stops there: the cortex is a ROSTER mechanism built for a
variable set; =consume= entries are called by name (=daemon.services.nlp=) and a dropped one is a worse
failure than a rejected key; =services= are processes. No recipe declares an optional consume key —
default only what a producer emits.

** the full patch was not full (row 6)

Declared complete with a table and a regex standing in for six files' hunks. beef's four-word probe
found it. The audit cost one re-read of six recipes (one shared shape, five sites each), one import
line, one README offset, and the honesty to name two decode paths the probes had not covered rather
than let a green suite discover them.
