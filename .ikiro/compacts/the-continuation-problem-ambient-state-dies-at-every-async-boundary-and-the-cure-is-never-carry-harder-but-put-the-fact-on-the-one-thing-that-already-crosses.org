#+TITLE: The continuation problem — ambient state dies at every async boundary, and the cure is never "carry harder" but "put the fact on the ONE thing that already crosses." The session's owner-drop was one instance; this is the class beneath it, and where else in the spine it lives.
#+TOPIC: a DEEPER fold under the session compact [[the-broadcast-owner-was-read-from-an-uncarried-ambient-while-the-user-already-rode-the-orm-context-carry-re-enters-so-the-intrinsic-column-sprawl-reverts-to-a-three-line-getfilterparams-read]]. Not the arc (that's told) — the general shape the arc was a shadow of. Written because =carry= "felt herrendously hacky" and beef's nose was right that something structural was unresolved; naming it keeps the whole spine (multiplex frames · pool drains · span · background jobs) from re-growing the same bug five more times.

* The class: a continuation cannot see the scope that spawned it
Every request handler runs in a synchronous-ish scope that sets up AMBIENT state — MikroORM's =RequestContext= (the em + its filter params), =shard.ambient= (the user), a trace span, a tenant. The language gives us =AsyncLocalStorage= to make that state reachable "for free" from anywhere in the call tree WITHOUT threading it as an argument. That is a bargain with a hidden clause: *the state is only reachable from continuations that are children of the =run(store, fn)= call.* The moment work is deferred to a continuation that is NOT such a child — a lazy generator resumed by a transport, a =Pool.drain()= awaited after the handler returned, a socket frame handled on the message-loop's own stack, a =queueMicrotask= / background job — the store is gone. Not stale. GONE. =ambient.getStore()= returns =undefined=; =getFilterParams= returns nothing; the span is detached.

This is not a MikroORM quirk or a Deno quirk. It is the shape of ambient-state-plus-deferred-work in ANY runtime. The handler's scope is a stack frame; a continuation resumed elsewhere is a different stack; ALS follows stacks, not logical requests. The bargain buys ergonomics inside the scope and charges a silent =undefined= at every boundary out of it.

* Why the session's bug is the pure specimen
A streaming response is the sharpest boundary the system has, because it INVERTS who drives. In a buffered response the handler produces the body and the scope wraps the production. In a stream the handler produces a SUSPENSION and hands it off; the TRANSPORT drives production later, pull by pull, from its own stack. So the body's side-effects — =em.create=, the =afterCreate= twitch, the broadcast owner-stamp — all run in the consumer's continuation, outside the producer's scope. The turn persisted (=inject=+=carry= re-entered the em) but the broadcast's owner read a DIFFERENT ambient (=shard.ambient=) that nothing re-entered. One boundary, two ambient stores, one of them carried. The drop was the un-carried one coming back =undefined=.

* The two cures — and why one is a tax and the other is the real move
Given a fact F the continuation needs, there are exactly two ways it survives the boundary:

** Cure A — CARRY the context: re-enter =run(store, fn)= at the boundary so the continuation becomes a child again.
This is =datamap.inject= wrapping the response body so every =next()= runs inside =shard.carry='s =storage.run(capturedContext, …)=. It WORKS and it is not a hack — it is the toll for lazy streams, paid once per boundary. Its cost is real but bounded: one re-entry per pull, and it re-enters ONE named context. The trap is thinking you can =enterWith= instead (persist the store past the scope) — that is the same idea without the scoping, and on a shared driver (one multiplex socket, many frames) it BLEEDS request A's store into request B. Carry is =run=; the footgun is =enterWith=. The session chose carry and marked it =// @beef hacky deep wire= — honest: it IS a deep wire, but a load-bearing one.

** Cure B — make F ride the ONE thing that already crosses the boundary.
The em is what crosses (=carry= carries it). The user is a *property of the em's filter params* (=bind("user")= set it, to scope find). So the user ALREADY crosses — it rides the thing that's carried. The broadcast never needed its own ambient; it needed to read the fact off the carrier. =repo.getEntityManager().getFilterParams("user")= — three lines. Cure B is not "add carrying"; it is "STOP maintaining a second channel for a fact the first channel already carries."

*The deep asymmetry:* Cure A is a tax you pay forever, per boundary, per context. Cure B is a one-time collapse that makes the tax cover the fact for free. The session's failure mode was reaching for a THIRD option — denormalize F into the entity (a =user= column) — which is Cure B aimed at the wrong carrier (the row instead of the context), and it sprawled because a row is written at N sites while a context is established at ONE. "too sprawlling" was the felt-sense of choosing the many-writer carrier over the one-writer carrier.

* The law that falls out
*Do not proliferate ambient contexts. Put every request-scoped fact on the ONE context that is already carried across boundaries, and read it from there everywhere.* The system had TWO ambient stores (=RequestContext=, =shard.ambient=) holding OVERLAPPING facts (both knew the user). Two stores means two carries means the boundary is only as safe as the store you remembered to re-enter — and you will forget one. Collapsing to one store makes "did we carry it?" a single, answerable question. =shard.ambient= is now dormant; that is not cleanup, it is the point.

* Corollary: routing that cannot resolve its target is a FAULT, not a warning
The owner-stamp is a ROUTING decision — which subscribers get this write. A routing decision that cannot be made means data is about to go nowhere. The old code =console.warn=-and-dropped: it treated an unroutable write as a diagnostic. That is the silent-fail beef hates, and it is not incidental — it is the SAME class one level up. A lost store produces an unresolvable route; an unresolvable route that continues is a silent drop; a silent drop is the boundary bug made invisible. So the corollary: *dispatch that cannot resolve throws.* This already holds in =steer.dispatch= (throws on no match) and =shape.strip=/=wire= (throws on ambiguous leaf); the reactive was the one router that whispered instead. Now it throws — which ALSO turns Cure-A-forgotten (a boundary you didn't carry) into a loud crash instead of a quiet gap. The throw is a tripwire on the whole class.

* Where else this lives in the spine (audit, not incident)
The same boundary recurs everywhere consumption is deferred past the handler:
- *Multiplex =Socket.#answer=* — already consumes iterable outputs INSIDE the carry (the =project_multiplex_transport= compact's "ALS / =/turn/close= fix structural"). The transport layer ALREADY learned Cure A. inject/carry is the identical move at the ORM layer. Two layers, one pattern — evidence the class is real and the cure is uniform.
- *EMITTER =Pool.drain()=* — buffers created in the drain are a deferred continuation; they route through the reactive too. They survived only because the drain is still awaited within the request scope (the em context still live). If any emit ever goes truly detached (a background refill), it hits the exact boundary — and now throws loud instead of dropping.
- *Span/trace* — a detached continuation with no re-entered span is an ORPHANED record (=project_span_reactive_spine=). Same boundary; the cure is the same shape (the span must ride the carrier or be threaded).
- *Background / scheduled work* — anything past the response has NO request scope at all; it must MINT its own context (fresh =RequestContext= + explicit facts), never assume ambient. That is Cure B taken to its limit: when nothing crosses, carry nothing — pass everything.

* The connoisseur reading
- *Reveal vs hide.* The owner was HIDDEN in an ambient store (implicit, un-carried, easy to forget). Reading it from the filter param REVEALS that the broadcast owner and the find scope are the SAME fact from the SAME source — the code now SAYS "routing uses the scope." Hiding it in a second ambient was the concealment that let the two drift apart.
- *The false binary was a pair.* "denormalize a column" vs "read ambient" both answered "where do we GET the owner." The dissolution was noticing the owner was already somewhere neither option looked: on the context we carry. When two options both feel wrong, they are often two spellings of the wrong QUESTION.
- *The thing that feels hacky is the toll, not the crime.* =carry= felt like the hack; it is the irreducible cost of lazy streams crossing async boundaries. The crime was the second ambient store. Feeling and fault pointed at different lines — the nose said "hacky here," the fix was "unify there." Trust the nose that something is wrong; do not trust its finger.

* Koans
- A stream is a promise the handler makes and the transport keeps. Whatever the handler knew, the transport must be handed — or the promise is kept in an empty room.
- Two contexts for one fact is one context and one bug.
- The fact that must survive the boundary rides the thing that crosses it. If it rides nothing, it dies, and it dies quietly.
- A router that cannot find its target must shout, because its silence is indistinguishable from success.
- Carry is a toll. Tolls are paid at the gate you built. If you are paying at two gates for one road, close one.
