* the entity catalog (learning daemon)

Shared repository =/entities/*= (NOT user-scoped): =mode= · =symbol= · =literal=.
Userspace =/userspace/entities/*= (user-filtered): =thread= · =intent= · =buffer= · =turn= · =memory= · =trace=. Identity: =user=.
The spine beef named: =mode→thread→buffer→turn= (session) and =literal↔symbol→memory→trace= (learning); Buffer is the join (carries =literals=/=symbols= m:n).

At pure runtime level: =literal·symbol·mode·intent= carry data; =buffer·thread·turn·memory·trace= are empty (need a user). beef: snapshot what's available "(not trace or memory)".

* the three vantages — the load-bearing LAW

Every entity read reachable three ways, and they are **byte-identical**:
- =mode= — =daemon.entities.<x>.find().toJSON()= (server MikroORM repo; what a mode sees via =ctx.daemon.entities=).
- =aperture= — =conn.call("/entities/<x>/find")= (raw wire POJO across =shape.http= + =shard.transmitter.inline=).
- =remote= — client =RemoteRepository.find= → =cast= through =RemoteEntityManager= (identity-mapped kind-instances; the shape the viewer binds to).

Because they're identical, ONE canonical file per entity (=entity-literal.snapshot.json=, =entity-symbol.snapshot.json=) written by the mode vantage; aperture + remote steps =expect(...).toEqual(canonical)=. The equality IS the proof of daemon↔wire↔client round-trip fidelity. (Earlier they were three separate byte-identical files = triplication bloat, collapsed.)
