* what happened, in one breath

A context load that turned into three separate investigations and one quest. First the m53 tree read
whole — the main quest, the pre-draft, the sibling V3 file, the five vdex snapshots — which confirmed
that r1 is still unlanded and that the four red steps are that one cause. Then he sent me at the
providers: /"find the actual data entities these providers return."/ Eight canonical docs later the
law fell out on its own — **every family has one RFC identity, N provider identities, and one provider
cursor** — and Proton turned out to have no API at all, only a Bridge speaking IMAP on localhost, so
it earns a preset and not a faculty set. Then he sent me at our own schemas, and the answer was worse
than drift: **there are three schema surfaces and they disagree** — typology's descriptors, MikroORM's
EntitySchema, and the =/entity/schema= skill that derives from the metadata at request time. Five
concrete divergences, and in the live italian database 2 381 of 2 593 symbols carry =traits: []= —
nameless rows minted by literal refs that never declared themselves. That report never got delivered:
two design handoffs arrived mid-turn and took the session. =V3 office-email= and =V3 office-calendar=,
read whole, then /"i want the application modes in detail."/ The dummy-data mode he asked for was
written once as a single =topography/dummy= and then killed by two words — /"one per"/ — and what
replaced it is better than what he asked for and better than what I first built: **one dummy provider
mode per office mode, owning both directions**, a demo dataset in and recording endpoints out, standing
exactly where gmail will stand. That is the whole point of having scouted the endpoints: the swap is a
static, not a rewrite.

* the providers, scouted (row 2)

Eight canonical references, fetched not recalled. What came back, in the shape the office needs:

| family   | the RFC identity   | provider ids                                   | the cursor                                          |
|----------+--------------------+------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| email    | =Message-ID=       | IMAP =UID= · gmail =id= · graph =id=           | =UIDVALIDITY+MODSEQ= · =historyId= · =deltaLink=    |
| chat     | — (none)           | =wamid= · =chat.id+message_id= · =channel+ts=  | offset · webhook · cursor                           |
| calendar | =iCalUID= / =UID=  | gmail =id= · graph =id=                        | =syncToken= · =deltaLink= · CalDAV sync-token       |

Chat is the odd one: **no RFC identity at all**, which is why the quest's provider-scoped chat slug
(=telegram.4521.918=) was right and stays.

Six findings that change the T2/T6 design, all now written into the v4 quest:

- =MESSAGE.external:{id}= is too thin. Every provider forces four: ={account, id, thread, etag}= — and
  =external.thread= is the VENDOR's grouping, never the threading edge. The edge stays the literal's own
  =uses=, minted from =References[]= · =context.id= · =reply_to_message= · =thread_ts=.
- **the sync cursor has no home.** It is per-account, not per-row: not a literal, not a static. Leaning a
  =sync= trait on the Mode row; nothing in v4 needs it, so it stays a fork.
- **flags are a set, not a field.** =labelIds[]= · IMAP keywords · =categories[]= all fan out;
  =message.status.*= covers three of them and the rest want =message.label.<slug>= symbols.
- **bodies differ at the wire.** Gmail hands base64url MIME parts, Graph hands rendered HTML plus a
  preview, IMAP hands a BODYSTRUCTURE to walk. ={text, html}= is the normalizer's product, not a field.
- **media is two-step everywhere** — WhatsApp, Telegram and Gmail all hand an id you exchange for an
  expiring URL. So attachments download at arrival into the mode's freight; lazy fetch is not available.
- **recurrence is the calendar's fault line.** Google and CalDAV both speak RFC 5545 RRULE text; Graph
  speaks a structured =patternedRecurrence=. Store the RRULE, translate Graph in its own service.

And the ruling-adjacent fact: **Proton has no public API.** The Bridge is a local proxy — IMAP
=127.0.0.1:1143=, SMTP =:1025=, STARTTLS, bridge password — so =proton/mail= is legitimate as a record
and carries ZERO vendor faculties. His /"email per provider"/ ruling stands for gmail and outlook,
which have real APIs behind calendar, labels and push; proton is the shared IMAP belt with other creds.

* three schema surfaces, and they disagree (row 3)

Not one schema. Three, with three different jobs and no reconciliation between them:

#+begin_example
typology  schematics/entities/*.js   → typebox JSON Schema (v.literal() · v.buffer() …)   App buffer declarations, cast/fill
runtime   EntitySchema (MikroORM)    → the actual tables; a domain EXTENDS them            the database
skill     daemon/skills/entity.js    → derived from em.getMetadata() at request time       what an agent sees
#+end_example

Measured divergences, typology descriptor vs runtime entity:

| entity   | the descriptor says                | the entity is                                       |
|----------+------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| =Mode=   | =installed: v.boolean()=           | =installed: string= — the content hash               |
| =Literal=| no =ontology=                      | =ontology: string=, and the subscriber derives it    |
| =Buffer= | no =status=, no =view=             | both columns exist                                   |
| =Thread= | no =phase=                         | =phase= enum, five values                            |
| =Intent= | declares =type=, no =user=         | has =user=, and **the table has no =type= column**   |

Why the drift is invisible: every relation in a descriptor goes through =v.rel=, which collapses to
=union([ID, object({}, additionalProperties)])= and discards the body. So outside =v.buffer()= — which
Apps really do declare — the descriptors are read as documentation and checked by nothing.

Live database state, both instances:

- vcompany: 33 literals (all =ontology: "vdex"=), 34 symbols, 162 pivots, 2 modes, 4 buffers all with
  =data: {}= — which is exactly why the harness prints no screen line yet.
- italian: 6 583 literals across =word= · =sentence= · =conjugation= (the =ontology= blank bug is HEALED,
  measured), 2 593 symbols — and **2 381 of them carry =traits: []= and =trait: {}=**. They are the
  =word.lemma.*= symbols, minted by literal refs that never declared themselves. 92 % of the symbol
  table is nameless.
- the same defect class in vcompany, one row: =vdex.format.md~=, bare, minted from a stray =README.md~=.
  Two bugs in one — the ignore list covers =*.bak= and not =*~=, and an undeclared format mints a symbol
  with no LABELED name. The T1.1 pass fixed this for COORDINATES and not for formats.
- =Mode.traits= is a comma-joined TEXT column (=EXPOSED,TOOLED=) while =Literal.traits= and
  =Symbol.traits= are json (=["DOCUMENT"]=). The =resolveTraits= raw =LIKE= works on both by accident.

* the designs, and the modes they became (rows 4–6)

Both files read whole through =DesignSync= (project =f7686382=), with =v3-data.js= as the fixture both
screens read and =support.js= skimmed as the dc-runtime. Measured before writing: =office/email= on the
shelf is a manifest plus a scheme file and nothing else; =office/calendar= does not exist; the landed V3
vdex App is the idiom to match — dapper tokens, =buffer/parts/=, one =call()= that logs every route to
the status bar and shows a refusal rather than throwing it at the screen.

Three rulings the designs forced, all taken as mine and overturnable:

- **=event= is a fourth literal ontology**, beside =vdex= · =message= · =transaction=. A span with a
  recurrence and a response state is not a message, and sharing =message='s traits would fork every read
  tool. A deadline is the same row with no =end=.
- **a series is a master with its RRULE, expanded in memory** for the range on screen. A 2026 week never
  touches a thousand rows; =occurrences.js= is the whole mechanism and a vendor service replaces its
  input, never its output.
- **the held send lands now.** The main quest said outbound was ungated until the officer stage; the mail
  screen draws the gate in the App, so it moves: the tool =reply= HOLDS (=MESSAGE.hold= +
  =message.status.held=), and =/key= — the only route that clears it — **is not armed as a tool**. The
  officer writes; only the human sends. Same asymmetry on the calendar: =create= and =move= armed,
  =delete= not. The gate is one route's absence, not a policy anywhere.

Plus one open fork of the main quest's, closed by both designs at once: **coordinates cross ontologies.**
A mail and an event both take =vdex.category.*= / =vdex.id.*= symbols, so one coordinate groups
documents, mail and the calendar, and every chip on both screens jumps into the vdex.

* the dummy, killed and rebuilt (rows 7–14)

He asked for /"a dummy-data mode - kinda like a fixture"/ and I wrote one: =topography/dummy=, a single
DATASET mode installing message and event rows, justified by the education precedent (a topology carries
a scheme, a topography carries the data over it). Two words killed it — /"one per"/ — and three more
turns rebuilt it into something the first cut could not have become by patching:

#+begin_example
office/vdex       ← mode.mountpoint (disk)          watcher                                    LIVE
office/email      ── provider ──▶ dummy/mail        /messages /send /flag
office/calendar   ── provider ──▶ dummy/calendar    /events /create /rename /move /delete
    ⋮ later, the same seam
gmail/mail · outlook/mail · proton/mail · gmail/calendar · outlook/calendar · caldav/calendar
#+end_example

What the rebuild bought that the fixture could not: the dummy **owns both directions**. In, a demo
dataset. Out, endpoints that RECORD — his /"mails sent, event created/renamed etc."/ — which is the
provider seam and the only place the office touches the outside world. The office names its provider in
a static (=provider: "dummy/mail"=) and reaches it at call time through =daemon.modes=; one line in
=daemon.js= swaps it for =gmail/mail= and nothing else moves. Type =dummy= is the register the vendor
families will share the shape of: **the dummy is the vendor called "none"**.

Two nouns kept honest along the way: =topography= was a real register to extend, not a coined one, and
dropping it cost nothing because the replacement is a better fit for the same family. And no trait, no
flag, no branch anywhere asks whether the dummy is mounted — the provider helper refuses by name and the
App shows the refusal, which is the same path every other refusal takes.
