* Intent

Expand survival corpus's conjugation coverage. Three concurrent moves on a single kernel:

1. Extend `present.indicative` bundles from 14 → 38 lemmas (add 24 new verbs)
2. Add three new tense bundles for the original 14: `past.indicative`, `imperfect.indicative`, `future.indicative`
3. Round out all 38 verbs with nonfinite literals: infinitive, gerund, past participle — fill the gaps where any of the three is missing in `verb.js`

Why now: 14 lemmas in present.indicative leaves the learner unable to construct most survival utterances with verbs they already recognize. Past + imperfect + future on the 14 unlocks past-narrative and future-plan speech using known stems. Nonfinites complete each lemma's lexical entry so reading material can resolve every form.

Why these new verbs: 24 lemmas selected by spoken-BR frequency × traveler utility, biased to `-ar` regulars for paradigm density, irregulars (ver, dar, dizer, conhecer) for pattern coverage.

* Triple-Check Protocol (per entry)

Every literal entry walks this checklist before it lands in `verb.js`:

| # | check                                                                         |
|---|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | slug matches naming convention exactly: lemma + verb-form + (tense.person.number) |
| 2 | `learning` is the conjugated surface form (not the lemma/infinitive)         |
| 3 | `learning` lowercase                                                          |
| 4 | `known` matches person + tense per criteria table (e.g. "I used to speak")   |
| 5 | `known` is English — no Portuguese diacritics                                 |
| 6 | `known` ≠ `learning`                                                          |
| 7 | `learning` form appears verbatim in `EXEMPLIFIED.learning`                    |
| 8 | `EXEMPLIFIED.known` reflects same tense + person as `TRANSLATED.known`        |
| 9 | example is unique to this entry (no copy across forms)                        |
| 10 | RANKED zipf/fpm/rank looked up via wordfreq for THIS surface form (not lemma)|
| 11 | symbol block contains every required slug from the inventory above           |
| 12 | aspect symbol assigned per Q-F decision                                       |
| 13 | functional/domain symbols match lemma map                                     |
| 14 | `proficiency.high-frequency` applied iff zipf ≥ 6.7                           |
| 15 | irregular conjugation hand-verified against BR-PT grammar reference          |

Pass = all 15. Fail = pull and rework before the literal goes in the batch.

* Generation Method

1. **Paradigm tables** — hand-curate full conjugations (all four target tenses + three nonfinites) for the 38 lemmas. Source: standard BR-PT grammar. Eleven irregulars hand-checked: ser, estar, ir, ter, poder, querer, saber, fazer (existing 14) + ver, dar, dizer, conhecer, dormir, sentir, preferir, perder (new). Two orthographic: pegar (g→j: pego), pagar (1pl pagamos straightforward; subjunctive pague — not in scope this pass). Regulars from `-ar/-er/-ir` paradigm rules.

2. **wordfreq lookup** — `python -c "from wordfreq import zipf_frequency, word_frequency; ..."` per surface form. Per criteria §RANKED:

   #+begin_src python
   zipf = round(zipf_frequency(form, 'pt'), 2)
   fpm  = round(word_frequency(form, 'pt') * 1e6, 1)
   rank = round(10 ** (9.0 - zipf)) if zipf > 0 else 999999
   #+end_src

   Each form looked up individually, recorded in the changeset table.

3. **TRANSLATED** — surface form on `learning`, EN with pronoun + tense on `known`, per criteria §Verb known conventions:

   | form              | EN pattern             | example          |
   |-------------------|------------------------|------------------|
   | past 1sg          | "I [V-past]"           | "I spoke"        |
   | past 3sg          | "he/she [V-past]"      | "he/she spoke"   |
   | imperfect 1sg     | "I used to [V]"        | "I used to speak"|
   | future 1sg        | "I will [V]"           | "I will speak"   |
   | infinitive        | "to [V]"               | "to speak"       |
   | gerund            | "[V]ing"               | "speaking"       |
   | past participle   | EN past participle     | "spoken"         |

   Surface-form rule strict: `learning` MUST be the conjugated form, never the infinitive.

4. **EXEMPLIFIED** — handwritten, unique per literal. Constraints:
   - PT sentence contains `learning` form verbatim
   - EN sentence uses matching tense + matching person
   - Real survival/A1 situational use (travel, ordering, asking, narrating, preferences) — no template strings, no copy-paste across literals
   - Lowercase except sentence-initial

   Sample bank (final wording during S4):
   - `falei` / "I spoke" → "Eu falei com ele ontem" / "I spoke to him yesterday"
   - `falava` / "I used to speak" → "Eu falava com a minha avó todo dia" / "I used to speak with my grandma every day"
   - `falarei` / "I will speak" → "Eu falarei com o gerente amanhã" / "I will speak to the manager tomorrow"
   - `comprado` / "bought" → "Eu já tinha comprado o ingresso" / "I had already bought the ticket"
   - `dormindo` / "sleeping" → "Ela está dormindo no sofá" / "She is sleeping on the couch"
   - `vou` / "I go" → "Eu vou ao mercado agora" / "I'm going to the market now"
   - `vi` / "I saw" → "Eu vi o ônibus passar" / "I saw the bus go by"

5. **Symbols** — per inventory tables above. Every entry walks the triple-check protocol.
