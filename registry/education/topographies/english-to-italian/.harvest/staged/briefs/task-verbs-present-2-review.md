## Critical

- **`chiedere` 2sg + 3sg — unidiomatic collocation "chiedere una domanda/e."** Italian pairs `domanda` with `fare`, not `chiedere` — "chiedere una domanda" is a well-documented English-transfer error (calque of "ask a question"); native Italian is `fare una domanda` / `porre una domanda`, or `chiedere` + a different object (`una cosa`, `un favore`, `il permesso`). Confirmed via Treccani's own grammar Q&A ("Non è corretto dire 'ti chiedo una domanda'... si chiede una cosa, un favore... oppure si fa, si formula, si pone una domanda."). Notably the file's own `chiedere` infinitive row already avoids this trap (`Voglio chiedere una cosa`), so the 2sg/3sg cells are an inconsistent slip, not a deliberate register choice.
  - 2sg: wrong `"Perché chiedi tante domande?"` → right, e.g. `"Perché chiedi sempre la stessa cosa?"` or `"Perché chiedi così tanto aiuto?"` (keep `chiedi`, drop `domande` as its object)
  - 3sg: wrong `"L'insegnante chiede una domanda difficile"` → right, e.g. `"L'insegnante chiede spiegazioni difficili"` or `"L'insegnante chiede un favore agli studenti"`

## Important

- **`spegnere` 3pl — gloss/meaning drift breaks paradigm consistency.** All 5 other present cells (1sg/2sg/3sg/1pl/2pl) + infinitive + participle use the "turn off (a device)" sense consistently (`I turn on`... `spengo il computer`, `spegni la radio`, `spegne la sveglia`, `spegniamo il motore`, `spegnete le candele`). The 3pl cell alone switches senses to "put out (a fire)": known `"they put out"`, example `"The firefighters put out the fire quickly" / "I pompieri spengono l'incendio in fretta"`. Both senses are legitimate Italian, but the CONJUGATED bundle presents all 6 cells as one paradigm table — a learner drilling `spegnere` sees five "turn off" cells and one unexplained "put out" cell. Fix: swap to a device-based example consistent with the rest of the paradigm, e.g. known `"they turn off"`, `"The workers turn off the lights before leaving" / "Gli operai spengono le luci prima di uscire"`.
- **`succedere` 2pl — strained collocation.** `"Succedete a una lunga tradizione di famiglia"` ("you all succeed a long family tradition") stretches `succedere a` past its normal register, which governs a person/role/position being succeeded (`succedere al padre`, `succedere al trono`, `succedere al direttore`), not an abstract noun like `tradizione`. The rest of the `succedere` paradigm (1sg/2sg/1pl on `padre`/`direttore`/`proprietari`) is well-formed by contrast. Fix: swap the object to a person/role, e.g. `"Succedete ai vecchi soci nell'attività di famiglia"` ("you all succeed the former partners in the family business").

## Minor

- **`conoscere` participle gloss `"met"` vs. infinitive gloss `"to know"`.** Intentional and linguistically defensible — Italian passato prossimo of `conoscere` with a person object is genuinely inceptive ("came to know" = "met"), correctly distinct from the present-tense continuous sense (`la conosco da anni` = "I've known her for years"). Flagging only for visibility since it's a per-cell semantic divergence within one lemma, same category as the two Important items above, but here the divergence is grammatically required rather than an error — no change needed.
- **`regalare` — parenthetical disambiguation `"(a gift)"` / `"(as a gift)"` on every cell.** Not in the criteria doc's documented convention list (which covers `(fem.)` for adjectives and `/` for dual-meaning words), but a reasonable and consistently-applied extension to disambiguate from `dare`. No change needed, noting for awareness only.
