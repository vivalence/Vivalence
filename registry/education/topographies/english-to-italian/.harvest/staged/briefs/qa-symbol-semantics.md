# QA Panel — Lens 5: SYMBOL-SEMANTICS

Scope audited: all `dataset/literals/words/*.js` (5,020 word entities), `dataset/literals/conjugation.js`, `dataset/literals/sentences.js` (no functional/domain/proficiency symbols present on sentences — out of scope), cross-checked against `dataset/symbols/structural.js` (the 50 `proficiency.*`/`functional.*`/`domain.*` slug definitions).

Method: parsed every word-literal file into structured data; verified every `functional.*`-tagged entity (353, full population) and every `domain.*`-tagged entity in the largest/highest-risk bins (`domain.mind` 192, `domain.shape` 139, `domain.state` 141, `domain.work` 112, `domain.entertainment` 104 — full populations); hand-audited **every** `proficiency.survival` entity (832, the lens's high-risk class) against the doc's own survival-vs-A1 boundary definition; cross-checked `proficiency.high-frequency` (221 entities) against each entity's own `RANKED.rank`; checked for symbol slugs used in the corpus but absent from `structural.js` (none found — no typo'd/invented symbols).

---

## Critical

- **`proficiency.high-frequency` violates its own definition for 128/221 tagged entities (58%)**. `structural.js` defines it as "Top-150 frequency — appears in virtually every sentence," but 128 tagged entities carry `RANKED.rank` > 150, including entities ranked in the tens of thousands: `dormire.verb.indicative.present.second.plural` (`dormite`, rank **36189**), `dormire.verb.indicative.present.first.plural` (`dormiamo`, rank 29404), `finire.verb.indicative.present.first.plural` (`finiamo`, rank 20056), `finire.verb.indicative.present.second.singular` (`finisci`, rank 11140), `venire.verb.indicative.present.first.plural` (`veniamo`, rank 8559), `credere.verb.indicative.present.second.plural` (`credete`, rank 6366). Rank distribution among the 221: 93 ≤150 (correct), 44 in 151–500, 47 in 501–2000, 29 in 2001–10000, 8 above 10000. Root cause matches the known failure mode the criteria doc warns about for `RANKED` data (lemma-level value copied onto every conjugated form) — except here it's the **symbol**, not the number, that got blanket-applied: the whole lemma family of a genuinely common verb (dormire, finire, venire, credere…) inherited `proficiency.high-frequency` regardless of whether the specific conjugated surface form actually cracks top-150. Fix: strip `proficiency.high-frequency` from every entity whose own `RANKED.rank` exceeds 150, or rewrite the description to describe lemma-frequency rather than per-form top-150 if that's the intended semantics.

- **`domain.state` is a catch-all — 46/141 entries (32.6%) are abstract nouns unrelated to "States of being and conditions."** The description covers physical/emotional condition (and correctly does: `stanco` tired, `felice` happy, `aperto` open, `rotto` broken, etc. — 95 of 141 entries fit cleanly). But 46 entries are miscellaneous abstract nouns carrying **only** `domain.state` and no better-fitting tag: `idea.noun`, `opinione.noun` (opinion), `decisione.noun` (decision), `dilemma.noun`, `programma.noun` (program), `tema.noun` (theme/topic), `verità.noun` (truth), `realtà.noun` (reality), `situazione.noun`, `condizione.noun`, `rischio.noun` (risk), `pericolo.noun` (danger), `sicurezza.noun` (safety), `successo.noun` (success), `destino.noun` (destiny), `responso.noun` (verdict), `quesito.noun` (query), `conclusione.noun`, `permesso.noun` (permission), `accordo.noun` (agreement), `abitudine.noun` (habit), `onestà.noun` (honesty), `discussione.noun`, `necessità.noun`, `possibilità.noun`, `importanza.noun`, `differenza.noun`, `obiettivo.noun` (goal), `risultato.noun` (result), `causa.noun` (cause), `vantaggio.noun` (advantage), `valore.noun` (value), `significato.noun` (meaning), `fortuna.noun` (luck), `bisogno.noun` (need), `scusa.noun` (excuse), `motivo.noun` (reason), `esempio.noun`, `modo.noun` (manner), `parte.noun` (part), `caso.noun` (case), `fatto.noun` (fact), `vantaggio.noun`, `dovere.noun` (duty). A learner filtering "Domain: State" expecting condition/mood vocabulary gets a grab-bag including "verdict" and "dilemma." Fix: split into the genuine state-of-being adjective set (keep as `domain.state`) and route the abstract-noun set to a more honest home (several fit `domain.mind`'s "thought, emotion, and perception," e.g. `idea`, `opinione`, `dubbio`-adjacent; the rest need either a new domain or removal of the domain tag entirely).

- **`domain.work` absorbed 29/112 entries (25.9%) of legal/judicial/civic-bureaucracy vocabulary unrelated to "Jobs, professions, and the workplace."** `giudice` (judge), `sentenza` (verdict), `processo` (trial), `testimone` (witness), `reato` (crime), `arresto` (arrest), `denuncia` (police report), `notaio` (notary), `pena` (penalty), `cauzione` (bail), `udienza` (hearing), `clausola` (clause), `verbale` (official report), `cittadinanza` (citizenship), `residenza` (residency), `anagrafe` (registry office), `rinnovo` (renewal), `iscrizione` (registration), `imposta` (tax), `regolamento` (regulations), `codice` (code), `richiesta` (request), `avviso` (notice), `protocollo` (reference number), `certificato` (certificate), `timbro` (stamp), `multa` (fine), `modulo` (form), `agenda` (planner) have nothing to do with having a job or being at a workplace — this is court/legal-system and civic-administration content. A learner filtering "Domain: Work" for office vocabulary gets "witness," "bail," "citizenship." Fix: same pattern as `domain.state` — these belong under a dedicated legal/civic domain (none currently exists in `structural.js`) or under `domain.travel`/`domain.city` alongside the already-present `passaporto`/`visto`/`dogana` bureaucracy items, not under `domain.work`.

- **`credere` (to believe) — all 34 conjugated forms tagged `proficiency.survival`**, contradicting the criteria doc's own Survival-vs-A1 boundary: "Survival = reactive, transactional... every word answers: can I get through this situation right now?" vs. "A1 = proactive, conversational... simple opinions." `credere.verb.infinitive` through every mood/tense (`credo`, `crederò`, `crederei`, `creda`, imperative `credi!`) is exclusively an opinion/belief verb — "I believe so," "do you believe in ghosts," "believe in yourself" — none of its example sentences are transactional. It is the one clear misfit among the corpus's 16 survival-tagged verb lemmas (andare, avere, dare, dire, dormire, dovere, essere, fare, finire, parlare, potere, sapere, stare, venire, volere all plausibly serve day-one needs; `credere` does not). Fix: remove `proficiency.survival` from all 34 `credere.verb.*` entries, leave them at their existing `proficiency.cefr.a1` tier.

## Important

- **`functional.modal` and `functional.auxiliary` are completely empty (0 entities) despite `structural.js` naming their own canonical members.** `functional.modal` description: "Modality — potere, dovere, volere." `functional.auxiliary` description: "Auxiliary constructions — essere and avere in compound tenses." All 167 word-literal entries across these five lemmas (`essere` 34, `avere` 34, `potere` 33, `volere` 33, `dovere` 33) carry an empty `symbols` functional slot — none of them have any `functional.*` tag at all. Two entire taxonomy branches are dead weight; any filtering feature keyed on "practice modal verbs" or "practice auxiliary constructions" would return zero results. Fix: tag the `potere`/`dovere`/`volere` entries `functional.modal` and the `essere`/`avere` entries `functional.auxiliary` (at minimum on the finite indicative/subjunctive/conditional forms where the modal/auxiliary function is live).

- **`functional.time`'s own canonical example is absent from the family.** Description: "Temporal adverbs — poi, adesso, domani." `poi` and `adesso` are correctly tagged `functional.time`, but `domani.adverb` (tomorrow) — named explicitly in the description — carries only `domain.time`, never `functional.time`. Same pattern for `oggi` (today), `ieri` (yesterday), `stasera` (this evening), `stamattina` (this morning), `stanotte` (tonight): all pure temporal adverbs, all `domain.time`-only. The functional/domain split for time adverbs is inconsistent — deictic day-adverbs got routed to domain only, sequencing/frequency adverbs (`spesso`, `subito`, `appena`…) got routed to functional only — and the description was never updated to match. Fix: either add `functional.time` to `domani`/`oggi`/`ieri`/`stasera`/`stamattina`/`stanotte` to match the description's own example, or rewrite the description to drop "domani" and state the actual rule (deictic day-adverbs are domain-only).

- **`functional.degree` vs `functional.intensifier` split inconsistently among near-synonyms with no principled rule.** `totalmente` (totally) → `functional.degree`, but its near-perfect synonym `completamente` (completely) → `functional.intensifier`. `estremamente` (extremely) → `degree`, but `assolutamente` (absolutely) → `intensifier`. `fortemente` (strongly) → `degree`, but `decisamente` (definitely) / `realmente` (really) → `intensifier`. The two descriptions ("Degree and intensity — molto, poco, più, tanto" vs. "Intensifiers — davvero, proprio, assai") don't articulate a rule that would actually separate these two synonym clusters, and the corpus doesn't apply one either. Fix: either merge the two families (the descriptions overlap enough that the split isn't earning its keep) or write down the actual distinguishing rule and re-sort the ~15 borderline `-mente` adverbs against it.

- **`domain.mind` + `proficiency.survival` co-tagging: personality-descriptor adjectives tagged as day-one essentials.** `simpatico`/`simpatica` (nice/likeable), `gentile` (kind), `divertente` (fun/funny), `intelligente` (intelligent) are tagged both `domain.mind` and `proficiency.survival`. These are social/opinion adjectives for describing people — squarely the doc's own A1 definition ("proactive, conversational... simple opinions") — not "bare minimum to operate... navigate, order food, handle emergencies." Fix: drop `proficiency.survival` from these five entities, leave them at `proficiency.cefr.a1`.

## Minor

- **`functional.discourse` drift toward formal-register stance adverbs.** Description: "Discourse particles — allora, insomma, ecco" (present and correctly tagged). But ~10 of the 29 tagged entries are academic-register epistemic/stance adverbs rather than conversational particles: `tecnicamente` (technically), `teoricamente` (theoretically), `storicamente` (historically), `ufficialmente` (officially), `apparentemente` (apparently), `effettivamente` (actually), `sinceramente` (sincerely), `onestamente` (honestly), `francamente` (frankly), `personalmente` (personally). These are a different linguistic category (sentence-level stance/disjunct adverbs) than `allora`/`insomma`/`ecco`-style discourse particles, and the family description doesn't mention them. Not misleading enough to be Important — the words are still discourse-adjacent — but the family has become a catch-all for "-mente adverb that didn't fit elsewhere."

- **A handful of low-utility household-object nouns tagged `proficiency.survival` dilute the "bare minimum for day one" contract.** `agenda.noun` (planner), `elastico.noun` (rubber band), `adesivo.noun` (sticker), `portachiavi.noun` (keychain), `portamonete.noun` (coin purse), `etichetta.noun` (label), `gettone.noun` (token) sit alongside genuinely essential survival travel/health items (`passaporto`, `farmacia`, `ambulanza`, `dogana`) in the same tier, but none of them answer "can I get through this situation right now?" the way the rest of the survival set does. Low confidence / stylistic judgment call, not clearly wrong — flagging for awareness rather than requesting a hard fix.
